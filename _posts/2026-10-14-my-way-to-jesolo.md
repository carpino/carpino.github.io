# In the Middle of My Life

As I write this post I'm 33, and I can honestly say I haven't been very active over the past seven years. In my early 20s I did some bouldering and rowing, and every now and then I'd go for a short run or lift weights. Back then I weighed around 78 kg, but everything changed in 2019, when I was diagnosed with depression.

From that moment on I started to gain weight and dropped all the sports I used to do. By January 2026 I weighed 118 kg, and at 33 I had high cholesterol, high blood pressure and elevated liver enzymes (ALT and AST).

Between 2019 and 2026 my focus was almost entirely on my mind, and hardly at all on my body.

According to Professor Paul Raymond Gilbert, the founder of Compassion Focused Therapy, our emotional life is shaped by three systems.

<img src="/assets/images/3emotions.jpg" alt="The three emotion regulation systems" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

The green system is active when we feel calm, reassured and safe: our lives are warmed by genuine connection, we aren't striving for anything, and we're content with what we have (endogenous opioids). The blue system is the dopaminergic one: it's active when we feel excited and motivated, seeking rewards such as status, goals, or reaching the athletic performance we were aiming for (dopamine). The red system scans for threats and reacts to them; it switches on when we don't feel safe (cortisol). When these systems are out of balance, they can contribute to psychopathology.

During those years I was living in the red system, and I almost completely missed the emotions and states of the blue and green ones.

Then, in February 2026, something changed.

# A New Beginning

February 2026 was a new beginning.

I started to see cycling as a way to explore nature and new places: mountain passes, small mountain villages, lakes, valleys...

Cycling is also gentler on the joints than other cardio sports, and easier to get into than running, at least over short distances with little elevation gain. Cycling performance is also easy to measure, so you can collect data and use it to compare yourself with... yourself.

When I started, my idea was to ride the same routes over and over again, trying to set a personal best each time. I was seeking the thrill of a good performance without harming any living being: the blue system!

And I remembered the calm and relaxation that come after physical effort, regardless of the result, thanks to endogenous opioids: the green system!

So I bought my first road bike, a budget Triban RC 500.

<img src="/assets/images/triban500.jpg" alt="Triban RC 500" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

I soon realised that, at my latitude, February is not the best month to start cycling outdoors. Shortly afterwards, my first smart trainer arrived: a Wahoo Kickr Core 2.

That was the beginning.

# Cicloturistica Città di Jesolo

It was around that time that I started thinking about having a target event for the year, a goal to look forward to.

That goal turned out to be the Cicloturistica della Città di Jesolo: a non-competitive ride held on 4 October 2026 in the provinces of Venice and Treviso, 76 km long with practically no elevation gain.

The lack of climbing was one of the main reasons I chose it, together with the date, which was comfortably far from February.

At the beginning 76 km seemed like a lot, but it looked like a realistic goal with enough time to train for it.

In early October the weather in this part of Italy is usually cool, neither cold nor hot: just perfect for outdoor sport. That was another point in favour of this choice.

<img src="/assets/images/cicloturistica_jesolo_planimetria.jpg" alt="Route map of the Cicloturistica Città di Jesolo" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

# First Steps in Indoor Cycling

So I started training on my Kickr Core 2. At first I used Wahoo's feature that simulates real roads by reproducing their gradients. Probably because I couldn't shift gears on the bike (and, of course, because of my low fitness level), it caused me some problems with my right knee.

From then on I set the gradient to 0%, completely flat, and my knee hasn't bothered me since.

This was my first training session in February: despite its short duration (26 minutes) and low average power (41 W), my average heart rate was 133 bpm.

<img src="/assets/images/febbraio_indoor.png" alt="Indoor training session in February" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

A few months later, in May, the numbers had improved a lot. They were still modest for a trained cyclist, but to me they showed I was getting better.

Almost double the power and duration, with an average heart rate 5 bpm lower than before.

<img src="/assets/images/maggio_indoor.png" alt="Indoor training session in May" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

But now it was time to take the bike out on the road.

# First Steps in Road Cycling

First of all, this is where I live: Alpago, in the province of Belluno, about an hour's drive from Venice.
And this is the lake I trained around all season: Lake Santa Croce.

<img src="/assets/images/santa_croce.JPEG" alt="Lake Santa Croce" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

This was one of my first rides.

<img src="/assets/images/22_Maggio.PNG" alt="First road cycling session" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

My average heart rate shows how tiring this session was for me, yet my speed was very low: around 15 km/h over a total of about 15 km.

I clearly remember that I was barely able to finish, and that I didn't have the energy for a single extra metre. In fact, I had to take a short break before the end just to make it back home, where I had started.

Almost a month later, towards the end of June, there were good signs of improvement.

<img src="/assets/images/27_Giugno.PNG" alt="Road cycling session in June" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

This became my training route for the whole of June, July and August. To be precise, the route was usually a bit longer, around 25 km: a loop of the lake, then on to Cadola and back through Paludi.

That summer my average speed was usually between 20 and 23 km/h.

Then, on 8 August, came a great day.

<img src="/assets/images/8_Agosto.PNG" alt="Road cycling session in August" width="500" style="display:block; margin:0 auto;">

74.96 km in under four hours. I had essentially reached my goal two months ahead of time. For the rest of the season I kept training on the 25 km loop, and I did one last 75 km ride a month before the event.

# Weight Loss

Between February and October 2026 I lost 18 kg. It wasn't all down to exercise: I also followed a structured meal plan, with monthly check-ins with a dietitian near my home.

My meal plan was around 2,000 kcal a day. I didn't follow it strictly the whole time, but combined with the exercise it helped me lose 18 kg, bringing my weight in October 2026 to exactly 100 kg.

Each meal was made up of roughly half vegetables, a quarter carbohydrates and a quarter protein (by weight).

I sometimes struggled not to add extra snacks in the afternoon or after dinner, but maybe that was my body asking for more fuel to support the training.
